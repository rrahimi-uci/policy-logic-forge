\section{Introduction}

Compliance systems must answer two different questions. First, what does a
source document say, and where is the evidence? Second, what does an
implemented decision artifact do on an input? Current text-to-logic pipelines
frequently conflate these questions: a fluent extraction is treated as a rule,
an executable model is treated as a faithful reading, and a passing unit test
is treated as evidence of quality. The resulting systems are hard to inspect
and their failures are hard to localize.

We study a narrower and more useful objective. Given a normative document, can
we compile a declared subset into executable logic while preserving provenance,
making unsupported constructs explicit, and measuring the two questions with
separate protocols? LEXEC (Lexically grounded EXecutable Compliance) is the
repository's answer. It combines a ten-stage extraction pipeline with a typed
\lexir, total fail-closed lowering, bounded decision evaluation, DMN 1.3
emission, solver/proof records, and an interactive review workbench. The system
does not claim to decide legal meaning. Instead, it defines the boundary at
which deterministic checks are valid and refuses to silently cross it.

This paper makes three contributions. (1) We specify an auditable compilation
contract: every retained rule has source spans, a stable identifier, a typed
representation, and either an executable lowering or a refusal code. (2) We
provide an evaluation instrument that separates source-evidence fidelity,
executable behavior, and reviewer agreement, with negative and unrun states
preserved in the artifacts. (3) We release an implementation and a concrete
experiment plan whose missing observations are enumerated rather than replaced
with proxy numbers. The distinction matters: the current fixture evaluator
contains only three labeled rules, and its output is a contract check, not a
population estimate.

The remainder of the paper defines the supported problem, describes the
compiler and review layer, records current engineering evidence, and states the
gates needed before any headline result can be claimed.
