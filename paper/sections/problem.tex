\section{Evidence-gated evaluation}

\subsection{Objects and claim boundary}

Let $D$ be a normative document, $S(D)$ its addressable spans, and $R$ the
candidate rules extracted from those spans. Each retained rule $r$ carries a
provenance packet $P(r)=(d,s,q,h)$: document identity $d$, source span $s$,
evidence text $q$, and content hash $h$. A bounded compiler maps a rule to an
intermediate representation or to an explicit refusal:
\begin{equation}
  C(r,P(r)) \rightarrow I(r) \;\cup\; F(r).
  \label{eq:compiler}
\end{equation}
Lowering is total over the declared IR. It returns an executable decision, a
structured validation error, or a refusal; it never invents a missing operand
or branch. For input $x$, execution $E(C(r),x)$ describes the compiled
artifact. It does not establish that $r$ is a faithful or unique interpretation
of $D$.

\subsection{Three evidence planes}

\textbf{Attributable source fidelity (AFS)} asks whether the cited source
supports the rule and whether modality, scope, exceptions, thresholds, and
dependencies survive extraction. Its anchor is independently annotated source
evidence. \textbf{Outcome equivalence (OE)} asks whether two declared artifacts
return the same result on a defined input universe and whether a pinned
independent engine reproduces the trace. \textbf{Operational evidence (OPS)}
records pipeline coverage, refusals, failures, latency, and resource use. OPS
can diagnose a system, but cannot substitute for AFS or OE.

\subsection{Coverage before conditional quality}

Let $U$ be the complete retained rule universe, $X\subseteq U$ the rules that
lower and execute, and $A\subseteq X$ the executable rules that agree with a
declared oracle. We report
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{yield}=\frac{|X|}{|U|}, \qquad
  \mathrm{quality}_{\mid X}=\frac{|A|}{|X|}.
  \label{eq:yield-quality}
\end{equation}
Reporting only the second term rewards selective omission. Refusals and invalid
artifacts therefore stay in the yield denominator and are stratified by stable
reason code. This resembles selective prediction's coverage--risk separation
\citep{geifman2017selective}, but the abstention here is produced by a language
boundary or failed proof obligation rather than a confidence threshold.

Each artifact has one state: \emph{measured}, \emph{exploratory},
\emph{fixture-only}, \emph{unrun}, \emph{blocked}, or \emph{invalid}. State is
not a quality score. It states what evidence exists and therefore what wording
is permissible. In particular, implementation and test coverage do not promote
an exploratory quantity to a scientific result.
