\section{Ethics, data governance, and limitations}

Compliance documents can contain personal, contractual, or commercially
sensitive information. The pipeline retains hashes and source-span references,
but redistribution is governed by the license of each corpus. Restricted
documents are never committed to this repository; manifests record access and
deletion obligations. Human annotators require consent, compensation, secure
handling, and a path to report harmful or ambiguous examples.

The system can make an unsupported interpretation look authoritative if its
refusal and confidence states are hidden. We therefore require visible
evidence, refusal codes, validation errors, and reviewer decisions. A compiled
decision must not be used as legal advice or as the sole basis for an adverse
decision. Model/provider changes can alter extraction and grounding, so model
identity, reasoning setting, and retry policy are retained in every run.

The main limitation is semantic scope. A typed, satisfiable encoding can still
misread a statute; an independent engine can agree on the same wrong model;
and a reviewer can miss an omitted exception. Our protocol makes these risks
measurable and recoverable, but does not eliminate them. Resource and licensing
constraints also mean that the present snapshot cannot support a population
claim.
