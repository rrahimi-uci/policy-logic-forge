\section{Limitations and societal impact}

\textbf{Scientific limitations.}
The present snapshot does not estimate population-level AFS or OE. The Dutch
anchor replay is unresolved, the privacy run lacks independent labels, the
fixtures are synthetic, and the cross-engine and human-review studies are
unrun. A typed, satisfiable encoding can still mistranslate its source; two
engines can agree on the same error; and reviewers can miss an omitted
exception. The protocol exposes these limitations but cannot remove them.

\textbf{Scope and generalization.}
\lexir{} covers a bounded subset of normative constructs. Quantification,
open-world context, jurisdiction-specific interpretation, and some temporal
semantics are refused. Results from Dutch regulatory models, contracts, or
privacy policies should not be pooled without domain- and language-specific
analysis. Model/provider changes can alter extraction, so configuration and
retry policy are part of each run identity.

\textbf{Data governance.}
Compliance documents may contain personal, contractual, or commercially
sensitive information. Restricted documents remain outside the repository;
manifests retain hashes, access terms, and deletion obligations. Human studies
require consent or an appropriate ethics determination, fair compensation,
secure handling, and a channel for ambiguous or harmful examples.

\textbf{Misuse.}
Executable output can acquire unwarranted authority. Refusal codes, evidence,
validation errors, and reviewer dispositions must remain visible in downstream
interfaces. A compiled decision is not legal advice and must not be the sole
basis for an adverse decision. The system is intended for auditable analysis
and review, not automated adjudication.
