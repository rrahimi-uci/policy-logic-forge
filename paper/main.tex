\begin{abstract}
An executable program can be internally consistent and still mistranslate its
source.  This distinction is routinely blurred when normative text is compiled
into rules, decision tables, or solver constraints.  We introduce an
evidence-gated evaluation protocol that separates source fidelity, executable
behavior, and operational reliability, and makes the evidence state of every
reported quantity machine-readable.  We instantiate the protocol in \lexec{},
a provenance-preserving bounded compiler with typed intermediate
representation, fail-closed lowering, bounded FEEL execution, DMN emission,
solver diagnostics, and an immutable review trail.  A retained audit of a
published Dutch decision-model benchmark reproduces only
\ReplayExactRows{}/\ReplayRows{} aggregate rows exactly; \ReplayMismatchRows{}
(\ReplayMismatchPercent{}\%) differ and are therefore quarantined rather than
promoted to an outcome-equivalence result.  A separate
\PrivacyFiles{}-document stress run demonstrates end-to-end observability but,
without independent labels, supports operational claims only.  These findings
show how evidence-state-aware evaluation exposes replay failure and selective
reporting that aggregate scores can hide.  The current artifact validates the
instrument and its audit behavior; population-level semantic accuracy remains
an explicitly gated experiment rather than an inferred result.
\end{abstract}
